精读方法（五步）

  1. 通读定主题：先读标题、首段与每段首句，用一句中文概括文章在讲什么。
  2. 分层读段落：标出转折、因果、举例、递进等连接关系，确认每段在全文中的功能。
  3. 拆解长句：先找主干主谓宾，再处理从句、非谓语、同位语和插入成分。
  4. 原文定位作答：题干关键词回到原文，答案必须同时满足语义、语法和上下文。
  5. 用译文复盘：做完再展开课件译文与答案；对照错题记录是词汇、结构还是逻辑定位失误。
口诀标题定向，段首定位；主干先行，修饰后理；题回原文，证据唯一；译文复盘，错因归类。

Passage 1 · Fall in Love with English（教材 P5）

Li Hua, a high-school student, was tired of his parents' constant nagging about his English learning. Every day, they would remind him to recite words, practice grammar, and do more exercises. This made Li Hua feel stressed and rebellious. In class, he found it hard to concentrate. The long lists of grammar rules seemed like a jumbled mess in his head, and new words slipped from his memory as soon as he learned them. His English teacher, due to the large class size, couldn't pay enough attention to his individual problems. As a result, his test scores were disappointing, which further frustrated him.

One day, his close friend Wang Tao noticed his low spirits. Wang Tao, who was good at English, decided to have a heart-to-heart talk with him. Wang Tao said, "Li Hua, I know you're struggling with English, but don't worry. English is not just about boring rules. It's like a window to a whole new world. We can learn about different cultures, watch amazing movies, and read interesting stories in English." He then shared some of his own learning tips. "First, try to find English materials that you're really interested in. For example, if you like science fiction, read English science-fiction novels. Second, make learning a habit. Set aside a little time each day, like 30 minutes, to practice English. It could be reading a short passage, listening to an English song, or watching a short video. Third, don't be afraid to make mistakes. Every mistake is a chance to learn and improve."

Inspired by Wang Tao's words, Li Hua started to change his learning methods. He downloaded some English podcasts about his favorite topics, such as basketball and astronomy. He also joined an English-learning club at school, where he could communicate with other students in English. Slowly, he found that English was becoming more and more interesting. He could understand the lyrics of his favorite English songs, and he even started to write short stories in English. As time went by, his English improved significantly. He was no longer afraid of English tests, and his scores gradually rose. More importantly, he had truly fallen in love with English, seeing it as a key to unlocking a wealth of knowledge and fun.

展开课件中文译文 · 爱上英语

李华是一名高中生，他对父母总是唠叨他学英语感到厌烦。每天，他们都会提醒他背单词、练习语法、多做习题。这让李华感到压力很大，还产生了逆反心理。在课堂上，他很难集中注意力。一长串的语法规则在他脑子里就像一团乱麻，新学的单词他转眼就忘。由于班级人数太多，英语老师无法充分关注他的个人问题。结果，他的考试成绩很不理想，这让他愈发沮丧。

有一天，他的好朋友王涛注意到他情绪低落。王涛英语很好，决定和他促膝长谈。王涛说：“李华，我知道你在英语学习上很吃力，但别担心。英语不只是枯燥的规则。它就像一扇通往全新世界的窗户。我们可以通过英语了解不同的文化，看精彩的电影，读有趣的故事。”然后他分享了一些自己的学习小贴士。“首先，试着找一些你真正感兴趣的英语材料。比如，如果你喜欢科幻小说，就去读英语科幻小说。其次，养成学习的习惯。每天留出一点时间，比如 30 分钟来练习英语。可以是读一小段文章、听一首英文歌或者看一段短视频。第三，别怕犯错。每一个错误都是学习和提高的机会。”

受王涛的话启发，李华开始改变学习方法。他下载了一些关于自己喜欢的话题，比如篮球和天文学的英语播客。他还加入了学校的英语学习俱乐部，在那里他可以和其他同学用英语交流。慢慢地，他发现英语变得越来越有趣。他能听懂自己喜欢的英文歌的歌词了，甚至开始用英语写短篇小说。随着时间的推移，他的英语水平显著提高。他不再害怕英语考试，成绩也逐渐提升。更重要的是，他真正爱上了英语，把它视为开启知识与乐趣宝库的一把钥匙。

词汇：nagging（唠叨；nag 的现在分词）· recite（背诵；朗诵）· rebellious（叛逆的；反抗的）· concentrate（集中注意力）· jumbled（混乱的；杂乱的）· frustrated（沮丧的；挫败的）· heart-to-heart（坦率的；诚恳的）· articulate（善于表达的；口才好的）· insight（洞察力；领悟）· profound（深刻的；意义深远的）
词组：be tired of（厌烦；厌倦）· slip from one's memory（从记忆中消失）· pay attention to（注意；重视）· have a heart-to-heart talk with（与……坦诚交谈）· set aside（留出；拨出）· make mistakes（犯错）· fall in love with（爱上）

长句分析（课件原解析） 1. Li Hua, a high-school student, was tired of his parents' constant nagging about his English learning. —— Li Hua 是主语；a high-school student 是同位语；was tired of 是谓语部分；his parents' constant nagging about his English learning 是宾语。
2. He then shared some of his own learning tips, saying, "First, try to find English materials that you're really interested in." —— saying… 是现在分词短语作伴随状语；其中 that you're really interested in 是定语从句，修饰先行词 English materials。
3. More importantly, he had truly fallen in love with English, seeing it as a key to unlocking a wealth of knowledge and fun. —— More importantly 是插入语；seeing it as… 是现在分词短语作伴随状语。
翻译练习（课件原题与原译） 1. Every day, they would remind him to recite words, practice grammar, and do more exercises.
译：每天，他们会提醒他背单词，练习语法，做更多的练习。
2. More importantly, he had truly fallen in love with English, seeing it as a key to unlocking a wealth of knowledge and fun.
译：更重要的是，他真的爱上了英语，把它视为开启丰富知识和乐趣的钥匙。

Passage 2 · Successful Language Learners（教材 P9-12；练习册 P56-57）

If we take a close look at successful language learners, we may discover a few techniques which make language learning easier for them.

First of all, successful language learners are independent learners. They do not rely on books or teachers; they discover their own way to learn the language. Instead of waiting for the teacher to explain everything, they try to find the patterns and the rules for themselves. They are good guessers who look for clues and form their own conclusions.

Successful language learning is active learning. Therefore, successful learners do not wait for a chance to use the language; they look for such a chance. They find people who speak the language and ask these people to correct them when they make a mistake. They will try anything to communicate. When communication is difficult, they can accept information that is inexact or incomplete. It is more important for them to learn to think in the language than to know the meaning of every word.

Finally, successful language learners are learners with a purpose. They want to learn the language because they are interested in the language and the people who speak it. It is necessary for them to learn the language in order to communicate with these people and to learn from them.

展开课件中文译文

如果我们仔细观察成功的语言学习者，可能会发现一些能让他们的语言学习变得更轻松的方法。

首先，成功的语言学习者是自主学习者。他们不依赖书本或老师；而是探索自己的语言学习方式。他们不会等着老师解释所有内容，而是努力自己去寻找规律和规则。他们是善于猜测的人，会寻找线索并形成自己的结论。

成功的语言学习是主动学习。因此，成功的学习者不会等待使用语言的机会，而是主动寻找这样的机会。他们会找到说这种语言的人，并在自己犯错时请这些人纠正自己。为了交流，他们愿意尝试任何方法。当交流有困难时，他们能够接受不准确或不完整的信息。对他们而言，学会用这种语言思考比知道每个单词的意思更重要。

最后，成功的语言学习者是有明确目的的学习者。他们想要学习这种语言，是因为对这种语言以及说这种语言的人感兴趣。对他们来说，学习这种语言是必要的，以便与这些人交流并向他们学习。

词汇：discover（发现；发觉）· technique（技巧；技术；手法）· successful（成功的）· explain（说明；解释）· conclusion（结论；推论）· purpose（目的；用途）· meaning（含义；意图）· communicate（传达；交流）
词组：first of all（首先）· rely on（依赖；依靠）· instead of（代替；而不是）· wait for（等待）· look for（寻找）· be interested in（对……感兴趣）· in order to（为了）· communicate with（和……交流）· learn from（向……学习）

长句分析（课件原解析） 1. If we take a close look at successful language learners, we may discover a few techniques which make language learning easier for them. —— 这是 if 引导的条件状语从句；which make language learning easier for them 是定语从句，修饰 techniques。
2. They find people who speak the language and ask these people to correct them when they make a mistake. —— who speak the language 是定语从句，修饰先行词 people，关系代词 who 作主语；when they make a mistake 是时间状语从句，修饰第二个谓语动词 ask。
3. It is more important for them to learn to think in the language than to know the meaning of every word. —— 句型为 It is + 形容词 + for sb. + to do sth.；It 是形式主语，真正的主语是 to learn to think in the language，可避免句子头重脚轻；比较级结构比较两者的重要性。
翻译练习（课件原题与原译） 1. If we take a close look at successful language learners, we may discover a few techniques which make language learning easier for them.
译：如果我们仔细观察成功的语言学习者，我们可能会发现一些让他们学习语言更容易的技巧。
2. When communication is difficult, they can accept information that is inexact or incomplete.
译：当沟通困难时，他们可以接受不准确或不完整的信息。
3. They want to learn the language because they are interested in the language and the people who speak it.
译：他们想学这门语言，因为他们对这门语言和说这门语言的人感兴趣。
4. 他们不是等着老师解释所有的事情，而是自己去发现各种模式和规则。
译：Instead of waiting for the teacher to explain everything, they discover all kinds of patterns and rules by themselves.

Passage 3 · Artificial Satellites（教材 P13-16；练习册 P58）

Many people dream of exploring the vast universe, and one of the most important tools for this exploration is the artificial satellite. Over the decades, countries around the world have launched countless artificial satellites with the aim of studying the Earth, observing the universe, and facilitating communication. At least several thousand artificial satellites have been sent into space in the pursuit of these scientific and practical goals. The first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, was the achievement of the Soviet Union. It was successfully launched in 1957, marking a new era in space exploration.

To make these satellites more efficient and versatile, scientists continuously improve their design and technology. Sputnik 1 was relatively simple in structure, mainly used to transmit radio signals, but it opened the door to a new field.

Sputnik 1 was not immediately recognized as a revolutionary invention in all aspects at that time, but it served as a significant starting point for the development of space technology. By 1960, many countries began to follow suit and develop their own satellite projects. Scientists used these early satellites to conduct a series of basic space research, such as studying the Earth's magnetic field and the radiation environment in space. Today, there are thousands of artificial satellites orbiting the Earth, and more than a hundred new satellites are launched into space every year. These satellites are used in various fields, including weather forecasting, navigation, and television broadcasting. Countless scientific papers and reports have been written based on the data collected by these satellites.

Today, although the initial excitement about the first simple satellites has subsided, the importance of artificial satellites in modern life has become increasingly prominent. Throughout the past few decades, satellite technology has evolved rapidly. It has become an indispensable part of modern society. The fact that more countries are investing in satellite research and development shows that the application of satellite technology is still expanding globally. No one would deny the great value of artificial satellites, but few people think that the original simple satellites like Sputnik 1 are likely to play the same crucial role in the future.

展开课件中文译文

许多人都梦想探索浩瀚的宇宙，而人造卫星是实现这一探索最重要的工具之一。数十年来，世界各国发射了无数人造卫星，目的是研究地球、观测宇宙并促进通信。为了实现这些科学和实用目标，至少已有数千颗人造卫星被送入太空。第一颗人造卫星“斯普特尼克 1 号”是苏联的成果，它于 1957 年成功发射，标志着太空探索迈入新纪元。

为了让这些卫星更高效、功能更全面，科学家们不断改进其设计与技术。“斯普特尼克 1 号”的结构相对简单，主要用于传输无线电信号，但它为（太空探索的）新领域打开了大门。

当时，“斯普特尼克 1 号”并未立即被视为在各方面都具有革命性的发明，但它成为了空间技术发展的重要起点。到 1960 年，许多国家开始效仿，开发本国的卫星项目。科学家们利用这些早期卫星开展了一系列基础空间研究，例如研究地球磁场和太空辐射环境。如今，有成千上万颗人造卫星环绕地球运行，每年还有超 100 颗新卫星被送入太空。这些卫星被应用于多个领域，包括天气预报、导航和电视广播。无数科学论文与报告都基于这些卫星收集的数据撰写而成。

如今，尽管人们对最初那些简单卫星的兴奋感已逐渐消退，但人造卫星在现代生活中的重要性却日益凸显。在过去几十年间，卫星技术发展迅猛，已成为现代社会不可或缺的一部分。越来越多的国家投身卫星研发的事实表明，卫星技术的应用在全球范围内仍在拓展。没有人会否认人造卫星的巨大价值，但很少有人认为像“斯普特尼克 1 号”这样最初的简单卫星在未来还能发挥同等关键的作用。

词汇：vast（广阔的；浩瀚空间）· universe（宇宙）· artificial（人造的）· satellite（卫星；人造卫星）· decade（十年）· launch（发射；发起）· countless（无数的）· pursuit（追赶；追求）· achievement（成就）· revolutionary（革命的；大变革的）· efficient（有效率的）· versatile（多才多艺的；通用的）· transmit（传输；传播；发射）· orbit（轨道；绕轨道运行）· forecast（预测；预报）· navigation（航行；航海）· indispensable（不可缺少的）
词组：dream of（梦想；梦见）· aim of（目的在于；旨在）· send into（把……送进）· in the pursuit of（追求；寻求）· open the door to（给……机会）· follow suit（跟着做；学样）· a series of（一系列的）· based on（以……为基础；基于）· invest in（投资于；投入）

长句分析（课件原解析） 1. At least several thousand artificial satellites have been sent into space in the pursuit of these scientific and practical goals. —— in the pursuit of 意为“追求”；have been sent 是现在完成时的被动语态，表示“已经被……”。
2. Many people dream of exploring the vast universe, and one of the most important tools for this exploration is the artificial satellite. —— 并列连词 and 连接两个独立分句；介词 of 后接动名词短语作宾语；后半句使用 one of + 复数名词 结构。
3. The fact that more countries are investing in satellite research and development shows that the application of satellite technology is still expanding globally. —— that more countries are investing… 是同位语从句，解释 The fact，其中 that 不充当成分、不可省略；谓语 shows 后接 that 引导的宾语从句，其中 that 可省略。
翻译练习（课件原题与原译） 1. Over the decades, countries around the world have launched countless artificial satellites with the aim of studying the Earth, observing the universe, and facilitating communication.
译：几十年来，世界各国以研究地球、观测宇宙、促进通信为目的，发射了无数颗人造卫星。
2. To make these satellites more efficient and versatile, scientists continuously improve their design and technology.
译：为了使这些卫星更加高效和多用途，科学家们不断改进它们的设计和技术。
3. These satellites are used in various fields, including weather forecasting, navigation, and television broadcasting.
译：这些卫星用于各种领域，包括天气预报、导航和电视广播。

Passage 4 · Emily and Innovative Prosthetic Technology（教材 P18-21；练习册 P59）

Emily is among the "tomorrow's people" who embrace innovative prosthetic（假肢的）technology to overcome physical limitations. She wears a state-of-the-art prosthetic leg that has changed her life significantly.

Now, Emily is an active member of her community. She often participates in outdoor activities, like hiking with friends. When she walks on uneven paths, she adjusts her posture with ease. For most people, walking and enjoying nature is a normal part of life. However, three years ago, after a tragic car accident, Emily lost her left leg below the knee, and such activities seemed like distant dreams.

After the accident, Emily fell into a deep depression. "I felt as if my whole world had collapsed. I couldn't accept that I would have to live with a disability," she said. But her strong-willed nature led her to seek solutions. That's when she learned about advanced prosthetic technology that could provide a more natural walking experience.

In a challenging surgical procedure, doctors prepared Emily's residual limb for the prosthetic attachment. Then, a team of engineers and prosthetists customized a prosthetic leg for her. The leg was equipped with sensors that could detect the movements of her muscles and adjust the leg's movement accordingly. At first, wearing the prosthetic leg was uncomfortable, and she had to relearn basic walking skills. "One month after starting the training, I took my first independent step with the prosthetic leg. It was a moment of great joy," she remembered. As she continued to practice, the prosthetic leg became more and more integrated with her body.

Today, Emily's prosthetic leg has enabled her to lead an almost normal life, but it still has its limitations. For instance, when she tries to run on soft sand, the prosthetic leg doesn't provide as much stability as a natural leg. Also, during long-term use, she sometimes experiences fatigue in the residual limb. But Emily remains positive. "I'm sure that in the future, prosthetic technology will become even more advanced, and I'll be able to do everything I want, like dancing and mountain climbing," she says with determination.

Passage 5 · How Junk Food Companies Make Their Products Addictive（教材 P23-25；练习册 P60-61）

Have you ever wondered why it's so hard to resist that bag of chips or a sugary soda? Many people don't realize that junk food companies use clever tricks to make their products addictive. When you walk into a supermarket, colorful packaging and catchy slogans draw you in. But what's really inside these products?

How do they make junk food addictive? Food companies add high amounts of sugar, salt, and fat to their products. These ingredients activate the reward centers in our brains, making us feel good and crave more. For example, a single can of soda may contain up to 10 teaspoons of sugar, which is much more than our daily recommended limit. Scientists have found that these substances can change the way our brains work, similar to how drugs affect addiction.

Why do companies do this? The goal of junk food companies is to make you buy more products. By making their food addictive, they ensure customers keep coming back. They also spend billions of dollars on advertising, targeting children and teenagers through cartoons, social media, and sports events. For instance, fast-food chains often offer toys with kids' meals, making unhealthy food more appealing.

What are the consequences? Consuming too much junk food can lead to serious health problems. Obesity, diabetes, and heart disease are all linked to a diet high in sugar, salt, and fat. Even if you exercise regularly, eating junk food can still harm your body. Many young people don't think about these risks and continue eating unhealthy snacks, not knowing the long-term damage they're causing.

Conclusion: Junk food companies use science and marketing to make their products hard to resist. Your health is valuable, and understanding these tricks can help you make better choices. While junk food may taste good, the danger is that it could harm your body in the long run. It's important to read labels, limit your intake, and choose healthier alternatives.

Passage 7 · Vincent van Gogh（教材 P30-32；练习册 P62-63）

Vincent van Gogh was a 19th-century Dutch painter who lived a life filled with struggles and loneliness. During his lifetime, he sold only one painting, and most people considered his art too strange and intense. In spite of this, he is now regarded as one of the most influential painters in history, with his works selling for millions of dollars at auctions. Along with Leonardo da Vinci, he may be one of the two most famous painters who ever lived.

Van Gogh was born on March 30, 1853, in a small village in the Netherlands. His family was religious, and his father was a minister. As a young man, he worked in art dealerships but later decided to become a painter. He had a close relationship with his younger brother, Theo, who supported him financially throughout his life. However, Van Gogh often struggled with mental health issues including depression and anxiety.

He didn't seem to fit into the traditional art world of his time. People described him as odd and unpredictable. Although he briefly studied art in Brussels, he was mostly self-taught. His paintings, characterized by bold colors and swirling brushstrokes, were very different from the realistic styles popular at the time. In his personal life, he faced many failures in love and friendship. He never married and spent a great deal of time alone, focusing on his art.

The time and place in which he lived were not favorable for an artist with such a unique style. Most art critics preferred safe, classical works, and they rejected Van Gogh's innovative approach. Rather than compromise his vision, he continued to paint passionately, often working long hours in isolation. From 1888 to 1889, he created some of his most famous works in Arles, France. However, his mental health worsened, and he famously cut off part of his own ear during a breakdown. He spent his final years in an asylum, where he continued to paint until his tragic death by suicide at age 37. Despite his short and difficult life, his art has left a lasting impact on the world of painting.

Passage 8 · Technology and Parental Involvement（教材 P34-38；练习册 P64-65）

Technological change is everywhere and affects every aspect of life, mostly for the better. However, social changes that are brought about by new technology are often mistaken for a change in attitudes.

Surveys on this topic suggest that parents today continue to be “very” or “somewhat” overly-protective even after their children move into college dormitories. If parents of earlier generations had wanted to be in touch with their college-age children frequently, would this have been possible? Probably not. On the other hand, does the possibility of frequent communication today mean that the urge to do so wasn't present a generation ago? Many studies show that older parents—today's grandparents—would have called their children more often if the means and cost of doing so had not been a barrier.

Furthermore, studies show that finances are the most frequent subject of communication between parents and their college children. The fact that college students are financially dependent on their parents is nothing new; nor are requests for more money to be sent from home. This phenomenon is neither good nor bad; it is a fact of college life, today and in the past.

Thanks to the advanced technology, we live in an age of bettered communication. This has many implications well beyond the role that parents seem to play in the lives of their children who have left for college. Passage 10 · Passenger Pigeons（教材 P40、P49；练习册 P68-69）

Passenger pigeons once flew over much of the United States in unbelievable numbers. Written accounts from the 18th and 19th centuries described flocks so large that they darkened the sky for hours.

It was calculated that when its population reached its highest point, there were more than 3 billion passenger pigeons—a number equal to 24 to 40 percent of the total bird population in the United States, making it perhaps the most abundant bird in the world. Even as late as 1870, when their numbers had already become smaller, a flock believed to be 1 mile wide and 320 miles (about 515 kilometers) long was seen near Cincinnati.

Sadly, the abundance of passenger pigeons may have been their undoing. Where the birds were most abundant, people believed there was an everlasting supply and killed them by the thousands. Commercial hunters attracted them to small clearings with grain, waited until pigeons had settled to feed, then threw large nets over them, taking hundreds at a time. The birds were shipped to large cities and sold in restaurants.

By the closing decades of the 19th century, the hardwood forests where passenger pigeons nested had been damaged by Americans' need for wood, which scattered the flocks and forced the birds to go farther north, where cold temperatures and spring storms contributed to their decline. Soon the great flocks were gone, never to be seen again.

In 1897, the state of Michigan passed a law prohibiting the killing of passenger pigeons, but by then, no sizable flocks had been seen in the state for 10 years. The last confirmed wild pigeon in the United States was shot by a boy in Pike County, Ohio, in 1900. For a time, a few birds survived under human care. The last of them, known affectionately as Martha, died at the Cincinnati Zoological Garden on September 1, 1914.

Passage 12 · Two Kinds of Popularity（教材 P40、P59；练习册 P72）

During the rosy years of elementary school, I enjoyed sharing my dolls and jokes, which allowed me to keep my high social status. I was the queen of the playground. Then came my tweens and teens, and mean girls and cool kids. They rose in the ranks not by being friendly but by smoking cigarettes, breaking rules and playing jokes on others, among whom I soon found myself.

Popularity is a well-explored subject in social psychology. Mitch Prinstein, a professor of clinical psychology, sorts the popular into two categories: the likable and the status seekers. The likables' play-well-with-others qualities strengthen schoolyard friendships, jump-start interpersonal skills and, when tapped early, are employed ever after in life and work. Then there is the kind of popularity that appears in adolescence: status born of power and even dishonorable behavior.

Enviable as the cool kids may have seemed, Dr. Prinstein's studies show unpleasant consequences. Those who were highest in status in high school, as well as those least liked in elementary school, were most likely to engage in dangerous and risky behavior.

In one study, Dr. Prinstein examined the two types of popularity in 235 adolescents, scoring the least liked, the most liked and the highest in status based on student surveys.

"We found that the least well-liked teens had become more aggressive over time toward their classmates. But so had those who were high in status. It clearly showed that while likability can lead to healthy adjustment, high status has just the opposite effect on us."

Dr. Prinstein has also found that the qualities that made the neighbors want you on a play date, including sharing, kindness and openness, carry over to later years and make you better able to relate and connect with others.

In analyzing his and other research, Dr. Prinstein came to another conclusion: not only is likability related to positive life outcomes, but it is also responsible for those outcomes.

"Being liked creates opportunities for learning and for new kinds of life experiences that help somebody gain an advantage," he said.

Passage 14 · Smart Buildings Against Earthquakes（教材 P66、P69；练习册 P75）

After the violent earthquake that shook Los Angeles in 1994, earthquake scientists had good news to report: the damage and death toll could have been much worse.

More than 60 people died in this earthquake. By comparison, an earthquake of similar intensity that shook America in 1988 claimed 25,000 victims.

Injuries and deaths were relatively less in Los Angeles because the quake occurred at 4:31 a.m. on a holiday, when traffic was light on the city's highways.

In addition, changes made to the construction codes in Los Angeles during the last 20 years have strengthened the city's buildings and highways, making them more resistant to quakes.

Despite the good news, civil engineers aren't resting on their successes. Pinned to their drawing boards are blueprints for improved quake-resistant buildings. The new designs should offer even greater security to cities where earthquakes often take place.

In the past, making structures quake-resistant meant firm yet flexible materials, such as steel and wood, that bend without breaking. Later, people tried to lift a building off its foundation and insert rubber and steel between the building and its foundation to reduce the impact of ground vibrations. The most recent designs give buildings brains as well as concrete and steel supports. Called smart buildings, the structures respond like living organisms to an earthquake's vibrations. When the ground shakes and the building tips forward, the computer would force the building to shift in the opposite direction.

The new smart structures could be very expensive to build. However, they would save many lives and would be less likely to be damaged during earthquakes.

Passage 15 · Attitudes toward AI in the Workplace（教材 P71、P74；练习册 P75-76）

When it comes to AI in the workplace, differences in attitudes are more significant based on factors other than professional background. For instance, younger employees are more likely to be enthusiastic about AI-enabled features, with 62 percent of those under 30 saying they welcome AI in the workplace, compared to 35 percent of employees over 50.

While there are signs that the acceptance of AI in the workplace is gradually increasing, an employee's professional background will have little to do with how AI becomes a standard part of work processes. Once AI technologies are fully developed and proven reliable, the early adopters will be the companies and individuals who can adapt quickly and make the most of these new tools.
